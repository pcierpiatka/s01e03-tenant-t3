parser · the shared parser reads day-first dates month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so 7 April becomes 4 July. This is the bug #4471 was opened for. It covers every channel and every tenant, and intake tags most of it right.

hint · booking-v3’s form says MM/DD/YYYY and reads DD/MM

Three tenants run booking-v3. Its date field tells people to type month first, then the form reads what they typed the tenant’s way. The symptom is the same swap, but the fix is in the form, not the parser: the kb’s own causeOf already keeps it apart, and the old page rendered it as “no ticket of its own”. Now it has one. It only reaches us through the form channel at those three tenants.

one-day · bookings shift by one day, no month flip

A small group of bodies show no swap at all: “Invite says Tuesday 12 May, calendar says Monday 11 May”, “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, a client in Lisbon and a client in Toronto holding the same meeting on different days, every June booking one day off. The desk’s notes already read them as a one-day move but filed them under parser by tenant. They are not a date-order bug. They look like a timezone or day-boundary bug, and a parser fix will not touch them. The desk’s note is the list we use, including R-41236 (“the invite arrived before I made the booking”), whose body shows no date. They stay filed under parser by tenant and are counted there, as the kb’s own table does; this ticket covers them as their own problem so the fix is not lost in the footnote.

How we counted

  • One id, one report: the export has duplicate rows, and uniqueById keeps one per id.
  • A blind reader per tenant classified every body by what it says, seeing no tag and no note.
  • Where the body and the kb’s rule disagreed, an adjudicator placed the report and a second reader re-read it with a seeded sample of the rest.
  • Bodies with no dates at all were placed by the kb’s rule for their tenant and channel.
  • Counts follow the desk’s own view, as exported: parser and hint split the 412 the way causeOf does, and the one-day reports are filed under parser and footnoted (readAs), so they are counted there and in their own ticket.
  • The table above is computed from the id lists at render time. The same lists feed tools/counts.py, which checks that parser and hint place every report exactly once and that every one-day report is filed under parser.
